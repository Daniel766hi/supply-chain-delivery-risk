How the twin works

Built from the same data and rules as the Python model in simulation/.

Flow per order

  • Each day resamples a real day's order count (Oct 2016 – Sep 2017) and applies the promotion multiplier. Every order gets a shipping mode, a lead time and a number of units from the real distributions.
  • The supplier ships up to its capacity each day, and goods reach the warehouse after the inbound lead time.
  • The warehouse ships up to its dispatch capacity, oldest first or premium first, and only while the units are in stock. It reorders daily up to (expected demand over lead time + 1 day) + safety stock.
  • On time = days waiting + lead time ≤ promised days.

Checks

  • With no promotion the Python twin reproduces history within 0.25 percentage points (30 runs).
  • With unlimited supply, the end-to-end model reproduces the dispatch twin exactly, run for run.
  • This browser engine matches the Python results on all 67 cross-checks: dispatch and end-to-end experiments, 500 runs each (web/check_model.js).
  • 25 automated tests cover extreme conditions (zero demand, zero supplier capacity, unlimited capacity) and conservation of orders and units.

Assumptions to keep in mind

  • Capacity is not in the data. 64 orders/day is the busiest day observed; treat higher values as what-ifs.
  • Everything upstream (supplier capacity, inbound lead time, safety stock, planning) is an assumption. The data covers only orders and their delivery.
  • Warehouse and carrier are one dispatch stage, and lead times don't stretch under load, so promotion results are likely optimistic.
  • Customers don't cancel when delivery slows. The source dataset looks partly simulated.
